W3Decide & Respond

NOIS3 RFTP ProcessRFTP, not RTFP

Turn a released RFTP into a decision, and a favourable decision into a compliant, priced, fully-teamed submission — on time, first time.

In scope

  • Bid / no-bid decision. The source says we write "on select RFTPs" but never says how one is selected. This gate is the single largest gap in the map — criteria proposed below.
  • Response coordination across internal teams; internal syncs.
  • Subcontracting and partnering negotiations — teaming intent signed before pricing, not after.
  • Technical writing and budgeting on selected RFTPs.
  • Consultant engagement; periodic internal reporting.
  • Review before submit. The portal takes one proposal per prime and a later submission silently replaces the earlier one — so an internal read-through is the last reversible step.

W4Deliver

NOIS3 Delivery

Deliver the technical outcome the task order bought, at a cost the client can defend, in a way that makes us the people they call next time.

In scope

  • Delivering technical outcomes against the awarded scope.
  • Value for money / client ROI — measured against a baseline agreed at kickoff, not asserted at close.
  • Trusted-advisor relationships with the client.
  • Milestone acceptance — each contractual deliverable reviewed and formally accepted before the next begins.
  • Escalation — schedule, scope, or participation risk raised to the portfolio lead within 24h? of being seen, not at the next status meeting.

W5Harvest

Post-Delivery: Knowledge Management + Product R&D

Close the task order cleanly, and convert what we learned into something the next pursuit can use — while the facts are still fresh.

In scope

  • Coordination with delivery teams, Fin Ops and others on successful task order delivery.
  • Close-out — final invoicing and reconciliation, deliverable archive, subcontractor close-out.
  • Retrospective within 10 business days? of close, written down.
  • Past-performance record written in the shape the next RFTP will ask for — scope, client, period, outcome, verifiable.
  • Reusable assets tagged into a library: challenge designs, evaluation rubrics, solver-community contacts, pricing benchmarks.
  • Product R&D — named in the title, absent from the workflow. No owner, trigger, budget, or decision forum. Either staff it or drop it from the title.

The feedback loop, specified proposed

The source diagram labels a "Workflow Feedback Loop" and stops there. A loop that isn't named, owned, and read is a label. These are the three returns worth drawing, and the forum where they actually get read.

W5 → W1

Past performance and client relationship intelligence sharpen the target list. What we can now prove we have done changes what we are credible bidding on next.

W5 → W3

Realized costs, pricing benchmarks, and reusable content improve the next response. Without this, every budget is built from scratch and every estimate repeats the last one's error.

W4 → W2

Delivery reality corrects the record. What a task order actually took in effort and elapsed time is the input to the next bid/no-bid call.

The forum

A quarterly portfolio review where all three returns are tabled and the target list, the bid criteria, and the pricing benchmarks are updated in writing. Owner: who chairs?

What the source doesn't answer

Ten open questions, ordered by how much damage the gap can do. The first four are decisions; the rest are definitions.

WhereThe gapWhy it matters & what to decide
W3No bid / no-bid gate "Select RFTPs" has no selection rule, so the choice is made informally under deadline pressure. Propose: domain fit with a reachable solver community; a named partner for every capability gap; staff available across the period of performance; purse-to-effort ratio inside an agreed band; and a Pwin judgement above an agreed floor.
All fiveNo single accountable person Every workflow names a team and three role titles. When two teams co-lead (W3, W5) and disagree at 4pm on a due date, nothing in the map resolves it. Name one owner per workflow and one tiebreaker for each co-lead pair.
W1 · W3 · W5No capacity model The Chase and Growth teams lead or co-lead four of five workflows. Three RFTPs landing in one week is a foreseeable event with no stated response — decide in advance what gets dropped.
W5Product R&D is a title only Half of the workflow's name has no activity, owner, trigger, or budget behind it. Staff it with a stage-gate and a funding source, or remove it so the map stops promising something no one runs.
W1 · W2 · W3"Discretionary" support is undefined Growth Team support is marked discretionary in W1 and W2 with no allocation mechanism. Who decides, on what basis, and by when?
W1 · W3"Periodic internal reporting" is undefined It appears in two workflows. Same report or different? To whom, how often, in what format? Undefined reporting is reporting that stops.
W2 onlyTooling named in one workflow of five W2 lists four systems; W1, W3, W4 and W5 list none. Delivery in particular has no system of record, which is where the KM handoff in W5 will break.
All fiveNo metrics Nothing in the source states what good looks like. The health signals in this map are a starting proposal — pick the ones you will actually read.
W4No escalation path A challenge with low solver participation or weak submissions is the normal failure mode of this business. There is no stated trigger, threshold, or route.
