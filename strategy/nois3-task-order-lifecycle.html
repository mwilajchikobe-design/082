The pipeline

Stages are work; diamonds are decisions. A task order only advances when someone answers the question in the diamond — today, four of these five answers are made informally or not at all.

Gate specification proposed

Each gate needs three things to be real: someone who answers, evidence that settles it, and a defined consequence for a no. A gate with no defined "no" is not a gate — it is a status update.

GATE AQualify

Is this task order in our lane?

Answered by
W2 portfolio lead, within 1 business day? of the RFTP being logged.
Timing
Immediately on detection — before anyone reads the SOW in depth.
Evidence to pass
  • The technical domain maps to a solver community we can actually reach.
  • The period of performance is feasible against current commitments.
  • No organizational conflict of interest, no disqualifying reps & certs gap.
On a no
Recorded as a no-bid in HubSpot with the reason. Reasons accumulate into a pattern — the domains we keep declining are the bench gaps W1 should be closing.
GATE BBid / no-bid

Do we commit real resources to this response?

Answered by
W3 co-leads jointly. Tiebreaker: who? — co-leadership without a tiebreaker stalls under deadline.
Timing
Within 3 business days? of release, so a "go" still leaves a workable calendar.
Evidence to pass
  • A named partner, with signed teaming intent, against every capability we lack.
  • Named staff available across the full period of performance — not "we'll find someone".
  • Purse-to-effort ratio inside an agreed band: band?
  • Past performance we can cite and verify. Unverifiable past performance is not a near miss in a federal proposal.
  • Pwin judgement above an agreed floor given the incumbent and likely field.
On a no
No-bid recorded with the reason, and a relationship follow-up assigned to W1 so declining is a choice the client sees us make, not silence.
GATE CSubmit

Is it compliant, priced, teamed, and read?

Answered by
submit authority? — one named person, not a team.
Timing
T−24h? against the portal deadline. This is the last reversible step.
Evidence to pass
  • Every field inside its character cap, verified in the portal, not the draft.
  • Attachments inside the stated file and page limits.
  • Price approved; subcontractor agreements executed, not pending.
  • A fresh-eyes read-through by someone who did not write it.
On a no
Fix it, or stand down. The portal takes one proposal per prime and a later submission silently replaces the earlier one — so there is no safe "submit now, revise later".
GATE DAward

Did we win?

Answered by
The client. The only gate we do not control — which makes what we do with the answer the whole point.
Timing
On award notice, logged by W2 the day it arrives.
Evidence to pass
  • Award logged, kickoff package assembled by W3: winning approach, budget, executed subs, milestone dates.
  • Delivery team assigned — at bid, or after award?
On a loss
Request a debrief where the vehicle allows one, and write the loss reasons into the W5 record within 10 business days?. A loss that isn't written down gets repeated at full price.
GATE EHarvest

Is this task order fully recorded?

Answered by
W5 co-leads (Chase + Growth, BIS), with Fin Ops sign-off on the financial side.
Timing
Within 10 business days? of final delivery.
Evidence to pass
  • Retrospective written down, not held in a conversation.
  • Past-performance record in the shape the next RFTP will ask for.
  • Final invoice reconciled; subcontractors closed out.
  • Reusable assets tagged into the library: challenge design, evaluation rubric, solver contacts, realized pricing.
On a no
The task order stays open in the record. Close-out is a state, not a date — and this is the gate that makes the feedback loop more than a label on a diagram.

Reading the two documents together

Neither view replaces the other. The workflow map is the org chart of the portfolio; this is its clock.

What this adds, and what it assumes. The source diagram contains no gates. Every criterion, threshold, and timing above is proposed here for you to accept or replace; amber fields are decisions no one outside your firm can make.

The one gate worth arguing about first is B. The source says technical writing happens "on select RFTPs" and never says how one is selected. Until that rule is written down, the most consequential resource decision in the portfolio is made informally, under deadline, by whoever is in the room.
