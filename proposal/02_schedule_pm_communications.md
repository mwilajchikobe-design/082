The task order begins with roughly six weeks of setup: finalizing rules with NCATS and NASA BPS, building the competition website, and assembling the reviewer roster from the NOIS3-039 quantum network and NAMs and toxicology channels. Stage 1 registration opens within eight weeks of award, with submissions due by mid-2027 and up to 25 winners announced by November 2027.

Stage 2 runs in two milestones: Milestone 1 submissions are due mid-2028, with up to 15 winners announced by November 2028. Milestone 2 submissions close in late 2029, with the grand prize winner and up to six runners-up announced by March 2030.

The convening for winners, runners-up, and advisors follows the final announcements, around May 2030. All solutions are available by May 31, 2030 per RFTP 3.1. Reporting continues through the twelve-month post-award evaluation survey and a final report, closing the task order by December 31, 2031.

Fixed internal lead times protect each public date: rules and rubrics approved before a round opens, reviewer recruitment and conflict screening complete 21 days before submissions close, scoring and reconciliation within 30 days of close.

Jay Chikobe, FedTech's Portfolio Lead for Innovation 15 years into federal innovation programs, is Program Director and the primary NIH and NASA contact, leading phase gating and quality assurance. A Senior Program Manager runs challenge design and judging coordination day to day. A Community and Outreach Manager leads recruitment and between-milestone check-ins. FedTech's SME and Judging Coordinator supports rules development, team formation, and convening logistics. Anthony Atala, James Yoo, and Shay Soker of the Wake Forest Institute for Regenerative Medicine (WFIRM) run the NAMs and toxicology review under subcontract. An Analyst supports the platform, registration, and reporting.

FedTech submits status reports quarterly per NOIS3 SOW Section 3.6, plus the pre and post-competition web forms and project data package this RFTP requires. Crowd engagement runs continuously, with pairing sessions, office hours, webinars, and a newsletter carrying the community between milestones. Two pre-submission workshops before Stage 1 and two before Milestone 1, a Q&A webinar each phase, and the public forum keep the community informed. All media products go to NIH for review at least ten business days ahead of use. FedTech runs status meetings at a frequency set at kickoff, trains the project team on any FedTech-run platform, and works with the CoECI Open Innovation Advisor on the close-out meeting.

The two biggest risks are recruiting into a solver community that does not yet exist and securing judges credible in both fields. Dual-track outreach through quantum and toxicology channels covers the recruiting risk. The named WFIRM bench covers the NAMs and toxicology panel. FedTech's own quantum networks, QED-C, and the DOE quantum information science centers cover the quantum panel.
