FedTech is executing NOIS3-045, a multiphase, multiyear NIH Common Fund challenge run out of the Office of the Director. The $1.089M task order administers a planned $13.1 million prize purse. It is FedTech's largest life sciences engagement, on the same methodology, the same vehicle, and the same NASA CoECI desk as this bid.

Under NOIS3-050, FedTech ran two technology surveys for NASA's Human Research Program on Spaceflight Associated Neuro-Ocular Syndrome. FedTech screened 107 candidates through one weighted rubric, with FAR Part 25 gates applied before scoring. The delivered answer was a ranked set of complementary measurements. Qu-SAFE asks the same question, whether an instrument can resolve a faint signal against a margin of error nearly as wide.

Under NOIS3-039, FedTech benchmarked quantum computing against NASA Earth Science Technology Office workloads, cataloguing about 140 organizations across the quantum field. Quantum gave those workloads no measurable advantage yet, so FedTech is on record telling NASA when a technology is unready, the judgment Qu-SAFE needs applied to competitor claims. FedTech also ran the 2022 Quantum Startup Foundry cohort at the University of Maryland, built quantum cohorts for DISA, and counts Infleqtion and Artificial Brain among its alumni. Those relationships reach the DC and Boston quantum hubs and the national labs inside them.

Anthony Atala and James Yoo of the Wake Forest Institute for Regenerative Medicine (WFIRM) led the two teams that swept first and second place in NASA's Vascular Tissue Challenge, taking the $300,000 and $100,000 prizes (https://www.nasa.gov/prizes-challenges-and-crowdsourcing/centennial-challenges/vascular-tissue-challenge/). The subcontractor on this bid has already won a NASA prize competition. Atala co-leads a BARDA contract worth up to $48 million over eight years running body-on-a-chip systems to model chemical injury and test treatments.
