FedTech and WFIRM bring directly relevant experience to every dimension Qu-SAFE demands.

FedTech has managed 136 programs, including multi-stage challenges and cohort competitions running six months to three years. FedTech is executing NOIS3-045, a multiphase NIH Common Fund challenge whose $1.089M task order administers a $13.1 million purse. Under NOIS3-050 FedTech ran two technology surveys for NASA's Human Research Program on Spaceflight Associated Neuro-Ocular Syndrome, screening 107 candidates through one weighted rubric with FAR Part 25 gates before scoring. Qu-SAFE asks the same question: whether an instrument can resolve a faint signal against a margin of error nearly as wide.

Under NOIS3-039 FedTech benchmarked quantum computing against NASA Earth Science Technology Office workloads, cataloguing about 140 organizations. Quantum gave those workloads no measurable advantage yet, so FedTech is on record telling NASA when a technology is unready, the judgment Qu-SAFE needs applied to competitor claims. FedTech also ran the 2022 Quantum Startup Foundry cohort at the University of Maryland; its alumni have raised $5.7B and include Infleqtion, which listed on the NYSE in February 2026.

Anthony Atala, MD, directs WFIRM, chairs Urology at Wake Forest, and is a National Academy of Medicine member. James J. Yoo, MD PhD, is Associate Director and an AIMBE Fellow, and leads WFIRM's biosensor program and NASA work, including tissue models flown to the ISS. Shay Soker, PhD, leads patient-derived cancer models. Atala and Yoo led the two teams that swept first and second in NASA's Vascular Tissue Challenge (https://www.nasa.gov/prizes-challenges-and-crowdsourcing/centennial-challenges/vascular-tissue-challenge/): the subcontractor on this bid has already won a NASA prize competition. Atala also co-leads a BARDA body-on-a-chip contract worth up to $48 million. WFIRM co-developed PhysioVerse, the open MPS ecosystem whose 5,000-plus datasets Qu-SAFE solvers can build on.
