FedTech is the prime contractor and performs $348,508 of the $462,493 total directly, 75.4%. The Wake Forest Institute for Regenerative Medicine (WFIRM) is the subcontractor, supplying Anthony Atala, James Yoo, and Shay Soker. Atala leads WFIRM's microengineered tissue model program and its national NAM standards work. Yoo leads its biosensor program and its NASA collaborations, including tissue models flown to the International Space Station. Soker leads its patient-derived cancer models. WFIRM already instruments its own tissue models for roughly twenty electrical, chemical, biological, temperature, and mechanical parameters, and is adding optical sensing.

This challenge needs three capabilities and the team covers all three: challenge operations at federal scale, credible NAMs and toxicology review, and reach into the quantum-sensing reviewer pool. FedTech holds the first and third; WFIRM holds the second, which no quantum-focused team could assemble alone.

WFIRM works on two levels. Operationally, the three run technical review in all three rounds. Strategically, they shape evaluation criteria and scientific content, supplying judgment FedTech does not hold in house. FedTech retains full responsibility for challenge design, the platform, outreach, communications, event production, and all reporting to NASA and NIH.

FedTech decides everything inside its own scope; the experts advise, and FedTech sets criteria with NCATS and NASA BPS. FedTech and WFIRM hold a weekly working sync and keep a joint risk register at each phase gate. NASA and NIH receive one consolidated report per milestone through FedTech as the single point of contact.

Atala and Yoo have committed to the roles described here. The firm-fixed-price subcontract executes at award, flowing down Section 508, FAR 52.225-5 designated-country, conflict-of-interest, non-disclosure, and data-rights provisions. WFIRM holds no direct relationship with the Government and no role in award determinations.
