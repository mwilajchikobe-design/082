FedTech proposes as prime contractor with two subcontractors. FedTech performs more than 50% of the work by value and holds full responsibility for cost, schedule, technical performance, and all Government communication. Both subcontracts are firm-fixed-price, executed at task order award under FedTech's standard subcontract terms, with defined deliverables, milestone-based payment, flow-down of Section 508, FAR 52.225-5 designated-country, conflict-of-interest, non-disclosure, and data-rights provisions. Neither subcontractor holds a direct relationship with the Government or a role in award determinations.

Subcontractor 1 — CIMIT (Consortia for Improving Medicine with Innovation & Technology), Boston, MA. FedTech and CIMIT have an established teaming relationship from prior NOIS3 work, which removes the ramp-up risk of a new partnership. Scope: challenge review and judging infrastructure, NIH-facing process guidance, and recruitment of preclinical toxicology and NAMs subject matter experts for the biology review track through CIMIT's existing biomedical expert network. Estimated value $93,000 (20%).

Subcontractor 2 — [QUANTUM PARTNER], [LOCATION]. Proposed; teaming agreement pending confirmation before submission. Scope: quantum-sensing and hardware subject matter expert recruitment for the hardware review track, technical guidance on rubric design for sensor performance and quantum-advantage claims, and hardware-side reviewer training. Estimated value $93,000 (20%).

The division is deliberate. The RFTP's stated barrier is the disconnect between quantum physicists, bioengineers, and preclinical toxicologists, and its SME requirement demands validation across that divide. No single organization we are aware of credibly covers both ends. Rather than claim coverage we do not have, we teamed for it: FedTech runs the competition and the cross-domain reconciliation, CIMIT anchors the biology side, and [QUANTUM PARTNER] anchors the hardware side.
