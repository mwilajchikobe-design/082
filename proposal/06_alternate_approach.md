The base bid delivers the full Section 2.0 scope inside the $465,000 NTE. This alternate prices the additional review depth Q-1270 and Q-1277 raise, and NIH can take either tier or neither.

Basis. Q-1270 sets a floor of three reviews per Stage 1 submission and four per Stage 2 milestone submission. FedTech sizes the panel against 40 Stage 1 submissions, 25 at Milestone 1 as new entrants replace non-advancing teams, and 15 at Milestone 2. These are pricing assumptions, not participation targets. A high proportion advances out of Stage 1 by design: it is a team-formation instrument, not a gate. The binding filter is Milestone 1, which requires an integrated sensor and a working data path. That is 280 reviews, 120 quantum and 160 biomedical. The $113,985 panel line carries them: $66,520 in honoraria at $193 per quantum review and $271 per biomedical review, and $47,465 in reviewer recruitment, conflict screening against the entrant list, rubric training, reconciliation sessions, and live-session attendance.

The gap is Stage 2 depth. A Milestone 2 submission carries a prototype data package, recorded test runs, and a live session. The base honorarium buys a competent read of that package and a scored rubric, which is what the SOW requires. It does not buy an independent recomputation of the calibration and limit-of-detection claims underneath it. That recomputation is the check that separates a real quantum advantage from a well-presented one, and it is the specific thing this challenge exists to establish.

Tier 1, $519,937. Adds $57,444 across the 160 reviews in Stage 2, Milestone 1 and Milestone 2 combined, raising Stage 2 honoraria from $37,120 to $94,564, about two and a half times. Reviewers get time to work the data package, recompute calibration and limit of detection against the claimed endpoint, and write findings the Government Judging Panel can act on rather than a score it has to trust.

Tier 2, $552,396. Tier 1 plus between-milestone support at six hours a month instead of three, adding $32,459 across the 63 program months, 189 additional hours. Q-1277 describes community management, forum moderation, platform maintenance, pipeline tracking, participant coordination, and periodic check-ins across a five-year period with long gaps between milestones. The base bid meets what Q-1277 describes; this tier raises the cadence, adding proactive check-ins and deeper pipeline tracking through the long gaps between rounds.

Schedule. FedTech proposes no change. The RFTP schedule works, and every deliverable lands by December 31, 2031.

What does not change. Both tiers keep the same scope, the same team, the same milestones, and the same fixed prize purse. The only variable is how much reviewer time stands behind each award decision. If NIH takes neither tier, the base bid still meets the Q-1270 review floor at every stage. If submissions run above these assumptions, FedTech raises it at the phase gate rather than thin the review.
