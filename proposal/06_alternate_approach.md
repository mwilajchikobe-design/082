This alternate approach addresses one requirement whose cost we believe the estimated task order value understates: the SOW's requirement to hire subject matter experts capable of validating both quantum-sensing hardware and preclinical toxicology/NAMs science. We propose our base approach at $465,000 and offer this alternate at $[ALT TOTAL] so NASA and NIH can choose between them with the tradeoff visible rather than buried.

WHY THE DELTA EXISTS. Standard challenge administration convenes one technical panel. This challenge structurally cannot: the RFTP names cross-disciplinary disconnection as a core barrier and then asks the contractor to validate work spanning it. Two coordinated panels cost roughly twice one panel — more reviewer seats, two rubric development efforts, two recruitment pipelines, two conflict-screening cycles per stage, and a moderated reconciliation step that a single panel does not require. Compounding this, the expert population is small. Senior quantum-sensing researchers and senior preclinical toxicologists both command honoraria well above the generalist reviewer rate, and the pool is thin enough that we cannot rely on volunteer or nominal-honorarium participation to fill seats across a five-year period of performance.

WHAT THE BASE PRICE BUYS. At $465,000 we deliver the full scope with a deliberately lean SME bench: [N] named experts per track, honoraria set at the low end of defensible market rates, reconciliation conducted virtually, and reviewer recruitment concentrated in networks we can reach without paid search. This is a real, executable approach. Its exposure is bench depth. If two reviewers withdraw mid-stage in a field this small, we absorb schedule risk rather than quality risk, but we absorb it.

WHAT THE ALTERNATE ADDS. The alternate funds a deeper bench ([N] per track), competitive honoraria at market rate, a standing reserve of screened alternates, and one in-person reconciliation session per stage gate. It also funds a documented validation protocol — calibration and benchmarking criteria agreed between the two tracks before scoring opens — which we regard as the single highest-value durable artifact this task order can produce. That protocol outlasts the competition and becomes reusable Government methodology for evaluating quantum-enabled measurement claims in preclinical contexts.

WHAT WE RECOMMEND. Fund the base approach if the estimated value is firm. Fund the alternate if NIH intends the SME validation function to produce evidence durable enough to cite in later regulatory or programmatic decisions. We are prepared to execute either and will not degrade the base approach to make the alternate look necessary.
